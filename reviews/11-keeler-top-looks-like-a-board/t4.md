## What is here that no T4 scenario names

- **`Theme::rank`, and `theme::NEEDS_YOU` beside it.** The rows are ordered
  by group, which is deliberately coarse: `failed`, `died` and `incomplete`
  share group 0. The header names them one at a time, in the state table's
  order, so it needs the table's own index — and it needs to tell two states
  apart to count them, which the group cannot do for a word it has never
  heard of. `rank` answers one past the table's last row for that word,
  which is what keeps an unrecognised state out of both halves of the line.
- **`Board::header` became `Board::age`.** Spec 10's one header line was
  the spec, the ref and the age of the answer; this task splits it in two —
  the first pair is the wave panel's title, and the age is what sits against
  the right edge of the line under it. What was left of `header` was a
  method with no caller, so it went rather than being kept alive by the one
  test that still read it.
- **A keypress clears what the last one said.** The scenario's second
  *Then* — "the footer is empty again after the next keypress" — and nothing
  in spec 10 did it: `key_said` was cleared only by a lever that had
  something of its own to say. `on_key` now opens with `says(None)`, and
  every lever below it speaks straight after.

## Deviations from the Implementation Notes

**The footer is yellow, and never red.** The notes say "yellow (refusals) or
red (errors)". `App.message` is one `String` with no severity on it, no
scenario asks for red, and the four sentences the board writes there —
three lever refusals and whatever a recipe or a read said — are all
refusals relayed in somebody's own words. A severity field would be a
second truth to keep in step with the sentence; if red is wanted, it wants
a scenario saying which messages are errors.

**Only the word carries the panel's colour on the wave title.** The
assumptions say "the title in the panel's colour"; the scenario says
`"wave" is blue`. The path after it is dim, because a title is read as a
label first and as a path second, and a path in the label's colour makes
the two one long blue sentence. "tasks" and the detail's task id are one
word each and are coloured throughout.

## The seven spec-10 tests this frame moves

The rows are inside a box now, so a line read back off the terminal opens
and closes with a border cell. `inside()` strips both and the trailing pad;
`row_of` sees past the left one; `heading_of` finds the column heading by
what it says rather than by which line it is on. What each test is about is
unchanged:

- **the header names the ref and the age of that answer** — the ref on the
  wave panel's title, the age against the right edge of the line under it.
- **a narrow terminal drops the detail pane before it drops columns** — at
  100×20 rather than 100×16: five running tasks and a heading are thirteen
  lines inside a panel where they were eleven bare. It still asserts every
  row whole, no clipped column, and no pane.
- **a long command is cut to the column with an ellipsis** — the second
  line is found through `under_of` rather than by searching for `└─`, which
  is also the first two characters of a panel's bottom border.
- **the detail pane shows the last five texts** and **j and k move the
  selection and the detail pane follows** — the pane's lines are matched
  inside the border rather than at column 0.
- **closed and waiting tasks take one line** — the line under a row is
  asked for at the indent the connector actually hangs at, for the same
  reason.
- **the finished view lists each task's title** and **a state's reason is
  never cut** — the column heading through `heading_of`.

## The generic review: three findings, all acted on

Run through the built-in `code-review` skill at high effort over `62f63ec`.

1. **A board with no tasks drew a panel about the task it has not got** —
   an untitled empty box, `┌  ────┐`, over nine blank lines, where the frame
   before this task drew nothing there. Real, and this task's making: the
   panel and its row are asked for together now. Test:
   `top::a_board_with_no_tasks_has_no_task_to_draw_a_panel_about`.
2. **Five builders were public with no caller outside the module.** `wave`
   and `title` are the whole of what a frame asks the panel for; `counts`,
   `needing`, `naming`, `strip` and `hints` are private, and the module's
   own paragraph about them says so.
3. **The first header line has no overflow rule** — where the second drops
   its hints whole, the first is composed and left to the panel to clip, so
   a narrow terminal with many exceptions loses the status age and ends on
   half an item. Left open, deliberately: the spec's assumption that names
   this — "when the first header line does not fit, the counts are cut from
   the left and the status age is never dropped" — sits in the width-bands
   paragraph, and **T7** is the task that applies those bands to `Columns`,
   the panels and the hints. Building a rule here would be behaviour no T4
   scenario demands, in the file T7 will rewrite. It is named here so that
   T7 inherits it as a finding rather than as a surprise.
