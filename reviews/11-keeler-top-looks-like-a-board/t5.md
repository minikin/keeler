## The two collapses, which the scenarios ask for separately

The spec says both of these, a page apart, and they differ in exactly one
row:

- *rows that outgrow the panel collapse to one line **except the selected***
  — the panel making room. The row somebody is reading keeps its second
  line, because what it is doing now is why they are reading it. This is
  also what makes the scroll scenario's arithmetic come out at T7: a panel
  eight rows tall holds seven rows, T12 takes two of them, and the six
  above it are T7 through T11.
- *z toggles compact by hand → **every** live row is one line* — somebody
  asking for the whole wave on one screen, watched row included.

`Collapse` is those three answers named — `None`, `Crowded`, `Whole` — and
`Collapse::takes(selected)` is the one line where they differ. A single
`compact: bool` would have had to pick one of the two scenarios to fail.

## What is here that no T5 scenario names

- **The hand answer wins in both directions.** `Some(false)` — a board
  expanded by `z` — is not collapsed again when a task starts and the rows
  outgrow the panel; it scrolls instead. The alternative is a board that
  undoes what the watcher just asked for, and the scroll anchor is already
  there to carry it.
- **The tasks panel moved from `frame.rs` into `panels.rs`.** The rows are
  still `frame::lines`'s, but which of them are drawn, on how many lines
  each and where the drawing starts are questions about the box they go in,
  and the spec's own task line puts them here ("the scroll anchor (`app.rs`
  and the tasks panel in `panels.rs`)"). `frame::table` and `frame::rows`
  went with it rather than being kept alive beside their replacements.
- **`viewing` in `app.rs`.** `j`, `k` and `z` are the keys that change what
  the board shows and nothing else; the levers are decided in `on_key` and
  carried out by the loop. Split out because `crap-delta` caught `on_key`
  regressing from 10.0 to 11.0 on the `z` arm, and because the two
  questions were already two.

## Deviations from the Implementation Notes

**`compact` sits on `Board`, not on `App`.** The notes say "`app.rs` keeps
`selected: usize` over tasks and `compact: Option<bool>`". `selected` has
been on `Board` since spec 10, for the reason this one joins it: the
renderer is handed a board and nothing else, so a view decision it cannot
read is a view decision it cannot draw. `App` owns the board and `on_key`
is still where the key is answered, which is what the note is about.

## The generic review: two findings, one cause, both fixed

Run through the built-in `code-review` skill at high effort over
`acd537c~1..HEAD`. It could not break the `room` arithmetic, `scrolled`'s
anchors, `adopt` carrying `compact`, or the newly reachable compact path in
`frame::lines`. It found this:

1. **The hint promised what the key would not do.** On a board the panel
   had collapsed by itself the second line read `z expand`, while the press
   set `Some(true)` and took the *watched* row's second line — the one line
   still there. Only the press after it expanded anything.
2. **And promised it where there was nothing to expand.** A crowded board
   of closed rows — thirty `passed` tasks in a short panel — has no second
   line anywhere on it, and still offered `z expand`.

One cause: the hint was a function of the render decision while the key is
a function of the stored `Option<bool>`. The hint now reads what the key
reads — `board.compact == Some(true)` — so the two agree by construction,
and `Tasks::compact` went with the coupling, leaving the tasks panel
returning its lines. Pinned by
`top::the_hint_names_what_the_key_will_do_and_not_what_the_panel_did`,
which fails on the old code at the first of the two.
